## 1. Game Overview

**Challengers** is a turn-based strategy game inspired by real space exploration programs and the engineering challenges behind them.

Players take control of historical space campaigns, managing limited resources, preparing complex missions, and responding to unexpected events. Every decision has consequences, and even the best-prepared missions can face unforeseen failures.

The game combines **strategic decision-making, resource management, and historical storytelling**, offering an entertaining experience that also introduces players to the scientific, technical, and organizational challenges of space exploration.

Through clear objectives, visual feedback, and meaningful decision-making, Challengers aims to make complex engineering concepts approachable without requiring advanced knowledge of space science.

### What Makes Challengers Unique?

- **History meets strategy:** Experience challenges inspired by real space exploration programs.
- **Alternative history:** Make decisions that can change the outcome of historical missions.
- **Risk and uncertainty:** Unexpected events can disrupt even the most carefully planned operations.
- **Meaningful trade-offs:** Balance time, budget, personnel, and safety while pursuing mission objectives.
- **Understand the consequences:** Discover how engineering and management decisions affect mission readiness, reliability, and performance.
- **Learn from real events:** Discover the historical background behind each mission and compare your decisions with those made in reality.

## 2. Core Gameplay

Challengers follows a **turn-based resource management system**, with weekly progression inspired by games such as *Faster Than Light (FTL)*.

Each mission has a limited time horizon, a fixed budget, and specific technical objectives.

Players must allocate their available resources across different departments, prepare their mission, and respond to events that challenge their strategy.

Mission dashboards provide **clear objectives, resource indicators, and department progress tracking**, helping players understand their current situation and identify what remains to be accomplished.

### Gameplay Loop

1. **Choose a campaign:** Select a historical space exploration program.
2. **Start a mission:** Review its objectives, constraints, and historical context.
3. **Select a specialist:** Choose a character with unique abilities that influence resource management.
4. **Allocate resources:** Invest money and assign personnel to different departments.
5. **Advance one week:** Develop mission capabilities and progress toward technical objectives.
6. **React to events:** Face unexpected problems, evaluate risks, and make strategic decisions.
7. **Complete the mission:** Reach a successful or unsuccessful outcome and discover its historical significance.

### Resource Management

Players must balance three main resource categories:

- **Time:** A limited number of weekly turns to prepare and execute the mission.
- **Budget:** A finite financial resource consumed through investments and decisions.
- **Personnel:** Available workers who can be reassigned to different departments each week.

Additional resources and technical constraints may vary depending on the mission.

In the initial playable mission, resources are distributed across four departments:

| Department | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Engineering | Solve technical problems and improve mission readiness. |
| Materials | Acquire and verify the components required for the mission. |
| Safety | Conduct inspections, evaluate risks, and improve reliability. |
| Operations | Coordinate personnel, procedures, and launch preparation. |

Each department contributes to mission readiness through measurable progress requirements.

**Resource allocation involves interconnected trade-offs.** Investing heavily in one department may improve its performance while leaving insufficient resources for other critical activities.

For example, prioritizing material acquisition may improve component readiness but limit the budget available for safety inspections and engineering analysis.

There is no single perfect allocation strategy: players must evaluate competing priorities and adapt their plans to the mission's requirements.

### Events and Decision-Making

At the end of each weekly turn, players may encounter events that interrupt their original strategy.

These events can introduce technical failures, unexpected expenses, safety concerns, or opportunities for improvement.

**Events involve probabilistic risks**, with their likelihood and consequences influenced by the player's preparation and previous decisions.

Players may also need to make difficult trade-offs, such as delaying a launch to address a safety concern at the expense of additional time and money.

Decisions are designed to provide understandable consequences, helping players recognize how preparation, engineering choices, and resource management influence mission performance.

### Mission Outcomes

Missions can conclude in three different ways:

- **Full Success:** The player exceeds the minimum mission requirements and unlocks additional historical information and scientific insights.
- **Partial Success:** The player accomplishes the minimum mission objectives.
- **Failure:** The mission cannot achieve its objectives due to insufficient preparation, exhausted resources, or critical events.

Regardless of the result, each mission concludes with a **post-mission analysis and historical debriefing**.

The report explains:

- **Mission performance:** Which objectives were achieved and which requirements remained unmet.
- **Decision consequences:** How resource allocation, technical preparation, and unexpected events influenced the outcome.
- **Historical context:** What happened in reality and how the player's decisions compare with those made during the actual mission.
- **Scientific and engineering insights:** The technical principles, historical lessons, and challenges illustrated by the scenario.

## 3. Historical Campaigns

Challengers is structured around real space exploration programs.

Each campaign consists of **sequential chapters focused on major technical and operational challenges**, allowing players to experience the evolution of a space program through strategic decision-making.

### Campaign Progression & Rewards

**Outstanding performance in a mission can provide strategic advantages in subsequent chapters of the same campaign.**

Achieving a Full Success may unlock bonuses such as additional starting resources, more experienced personnel, or improved engineering capabilities.

These rewards encourage players to optimize their decisions and create a sense of progression across the campaign, while preserving the uncertainty and challenges of future missions.

### Planned Campaigns

- **Apollo 11:** Explore the challenges involved in humanity's first crewed lunar landing.
- **Space Shuttle Program:** Manage engineering, operational, and safety challenges associated with reusable spacecraft.
- **Artemis Program:** Experience the challenges of modern lunar exploration.

### Initial Campaign: Space Shuttle Program

The first campaign focuses on NASA's Space Shuttle Program, examining the engineering achievements, organizational decisions, and technical risks that shaped its history.

Rather than recreating every individual flight, its chapters explore important challenges faced throughout the program.

#### First Playable Mission: Challenger — Launch Readiness (1986)

The initial mission is inspired by the preparations leading to the **Space Shuttle Challenger disaster on January 28, 1986**.

Players are responsible for managing launch readiness, coordinating departments, allocating resources, and evaluating operational risks.

Their decisions can influence whether the mission proceeds safely, is delayed, or ends in failure.

The mission explores how technical preparation, engineering communication, scheduling pressures, and risk management can influence critical decisions.

At the end of the scenario, players discover what happened historically, including the technical and organizational factors that contributed to the Challenger disaster.

## 4. Development & MVP

Challengers is being developed as a hackathon prototype, focusing on a small but complete gameplay experience.

### Technology

| Component | Technology |
|---|---|
| Game Engine | Unity 6 |
| Programming Language | C# |
| Genre | Turn-Based Strategy / Resource Management |
| Project | NASA Space Apps Challenge |

### MVP Scope

The first playable version focuses on **one complete mission within the Space Shuttle campaign**.

The planned prototype includes:

- Campaign and mission selection.
- Specialist selection with different gameplay abilities.
- Weekly turn-based progression.
- Budget and personnel allocation.
- Mission dashboard with objectives and resource indicators.
- Department progress tracking.
- Probabilistic events and strategic decisions.
- Mission success and failure conditions.
- Post-mission analysis with technical explanations and historical context.

### Future Development

The game's design aims to support additional historical campaigns and missions through a reusable gameplay system.

Future expansions may introduce new specialists, technical departments, mission-specific resources, historical scenarios, and more complex decision-making mechanics.
